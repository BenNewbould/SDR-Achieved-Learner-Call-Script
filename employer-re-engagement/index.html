Primary Objective

Primary Objective

Re-engage employers that have previously worked with Velocity Academy and identify opportunities to:

Upskill existing employees, particularly through AI & Automation
Upskill employees in IT, digital, marketing or business administration
Recruit a new apprentice
Book a Partnership Consultation with a Velocity Academy Partnership Manager

The objective of the SDR call is not to sell the apprenticeship in full. The aim is to identify a potential requirement, create interest and book the next conversation.

1

Opening

SDR
“Hi [CONTACT NAME], it’s [YOUR NAME] calling from Velocity Academy. How are you?”

Allow them to respond.

“I’m just getting back in touch because we’ve worked with [EMPLOYER NAME] previously through your apprenticeship training.”
Where learner names are available
“I believe we’ve supported people including [LEARNER NAME / LEARNER NAMES] at [EMPLOYER NAME].”
“I just wanted to check in really and see how things are going with you guys at the moment.”
2

Make It Conversational

Do not immediately start pitching another apprenticeship. Use the existing relationship.

“How have things been since we last worked together?”
“Are [LEARNER NAME / LEARNER NAMES] still with the organisation?”
IF YES
“Brilliant. How have they progressed since completing/starting their apprenticeship?”

“Have they moved into any different responsibilities or roles?”
IF NO
“No problem at all. Has much changed within the team since then?”
3

Reason for the Call

Once some rapport has been established:

“The main reason I wanted to get back in touch is that quite a lot has changed at Velocity since we last spoke, particularly around the programmes we're delivering for existing employees.”
“We're doing a lot at the moment around helping organisations upskill their current workforce, rather than apprenticeships necessarily meaning bringing somebody new into the business.”
“One of the biggest areas we're seeing employers looking at currently is AI and automation.”
4

AI Discovery

MAIN FOCUS

This should be the main focus of the conversation.

“Out of interest, how much is AI being used within [EMPLOYER NAME] at the moment?”

Follow-up questions

  • “Are staff currently using tools such as ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot or other AI tools?”
  • “Would you say that's something being actively encouraged by the business, or is it more individual employees experimenting with it themselves?”
  • “Are there any teams where you're currently looking at improving productivity or reducing repetitive/manual work?”
  • “Are there processes within the organisation that you know could probably be automated but haven't really been looked at yet?”
  • “Is AI something that's currently on the agenda for your management team?”

Interested? 5. Position the AI & Automation Apprenticeship · Not a priority? 6. Broaden the Conversation

5

Position the AI & Automation Apprenticeship

Use this if there is any indication of interest.

“That sounds very similar to the conversations we're having with quite a few employers.”
“We now deliver an AI & Automation Practitioner apprenticeship, which is designed for existing members of staff rather than somebody needing to already be an AI specialist.”
“The idea is to help employees understand where AI can practically be used within their role and organisation — things like identifying processes that can be improved, using generative AI effectively, automation, working with data and implementing AI solutions within the business.”
“So it isn't just learning the theory of AI. The focus is very much on applying it back into their day-to-day role.”

Qualification questions

  • “Who within the organisation do you think would benefit most from something like that?”
  • “Would it mainly be managers and team leaders, people working with data, operations teams, marketing, IT — or potentially across several departments?”
6

Broaden the Conversation

If AI isn't an immediate priority, do not end the call.

“No problem at all. AI is obviously only one part of what we deliver.”
“We're also supporting employers with apprenticeships for existing members of staff across areas such as IT, digital, marketing and business administration.”
“Are there any other teams at the moment where you're looking at developing people, progressing somebody internally or addressing a skills gap?”

Prompts if required

Marketing
“Do you have anybody responsible for digital marketing, content, social media, campaigns or wider marketing activity that you're looking to develop?”
IT / Digital
“Have you got anyone within IT support, infrastructure, applications, networking or cyber who you're looking to progress?”
Business / Administration
“Are there administrators or support staff who are taking on more responsibility and could benefit from structured development?”
7

Recruitment / New Apprentice Route

“The other side of it is recruitment.”
“Are there any teams where you're considering adding headcount over the next few months?”
IF YES
“We can also manage the apprentice recruitment process for you, so we can advertise the opportunity, screen applicants and send suitable candidates across for you to interview.”
“There have also been some significant changes to apprenticeship funding recently.”
“For eligible non-levy employers, from October there is a £2,000 hiring payment available when recruiting a new apprentice aged 16 to 24, so there may be additional support available if you're considering bringing somebody new into the organisation.”
“Would that be worth exploring?”
8

Identify the Opportunity

Area of interest
AI / IT / Marketing / Business Administration / Recruitment / Other
Potential employees
“How many people do you think potentially could benefit?”
Decision maker
“Would this normally sit with yourself, or would somebody else within the organisation be involved in looking at training and apprenticeships?”
Timescale
“Is this something you're looking at fairly soon, or more something you're considering over the next few months?”
9

Book the Partnership Call

PREFERRED OUTCOME

Do not finish by sending information unless absolutely necessary. The preferred outcome is a meeting.

“What would probably make most sense rather than me trying to go through everything now is to get you booked in with one of our Partnership Managers.”
“They can spend 20–30 minutes with you, understand what you're looking to achieve and then walk you through which programmes could potentially fit, how the funding works and what the next steps would look like.”
“Would you be happy for me to get something booked in?”
IF YES
“Perfect. Would [DAY/TIME OPTION 1] or [DAY/TIME OPTION 2] work better?”
10

If They Say “Just Send Me Some Information”

“Absolutely, I'm happy to send something across.”
“Just so I send you something that's actually relevant rather than a load of generic apprenticeship information, which area would be most useful — AI, IT, marketing, business administration or recruitment?”

Establish interest. Then:

“I'll get that across. It might still be worth putting 20 minutes in the diary with the Partnership Manager as well, even if it's just an exploratory conversation. If after looking at it you decide it's not relevant, that's absolutely fine.”
“Would [OPTION 1] or [OPTION 2] work?”
11

If They Say “We Don't Need Any Apprentices”

“That's absolutely fine.”

“Just so I understand, when you say apprentices, are you thinking mainly about recruiting somebody new?”

Allow response.

“The reason I ask is that a large proportion of what we're doing now is actually upskilling existing employees.”
“So, for example, somebody already working within the organisation could complete an apprenticeship around AI and automation as part of their existing role.”
“Is developing your existing staff something that's more relevant than recruitment at the moment?”
12

If They Say “We're Already Using AI”

BUYING SIGNAL

This is a positive buying signal.

“That's really interesting.”
  • “How are you currently using it?”
  • “Is that mainly people using generative AI tools individually, or have you started looking at AI and automation at an organisational level?”
  • “Have you got any formal training or governance around how employees use AI?”
  • “Are there processes you're actively trying to automate?”

Then:

“That sounds like it would definitely be worth a conversation with our Partnership Manager because the apprenticeship is designed to move people beyond simply using tools like ChatGPT and into identifying, developing and implementing AI and automation opportunities within the organisation.”
13

If They Say “We Don't Use AI”

“That's actually quite common.”
“Would you say that's because you haven't identified where it could be used yet, or is it more that it hasn't become a priority internally?”

Allow response.

“One of the things we're helping organisations with is identifying where AI could realistically save staff time or improve processes before they start investing heavily in different tools.”
“It could be worth having an exploratory conversation just to understand what's possible within your organisation.”
14

If They Already Have Another Training Provider

“No problem at all, and we're not necessarily looking to replace anything that's already working well.”
“Do they currently support you with AI and automation training as well?”
IF NO / UNSURE
“That could therefore be an area where we complement what you're already doing.”
“We work with organisations where different providers support different areas of their apprenticeship strategy.”
15

Close

MEETING BOOKED
“Brilliant. I've got you booked for [DATE/TIME] with [PARTNERSHIP MANAGER].”
“I'll send the Teams invitation over to [EMAIL ADDRESS]. Is that still the best email for you?”
“They'll be able to go through the AI programme, any other skills requirements you have and the funding options available.”
“Thanks [CONTACT NAME], really good speaking with you again.”

SDR Call Structure

The SDR should avoid turning the call into a programme presentation.

RECONNECT→PERSONALISE→DISCOVER→AI→WIDER SKILLS→RECRUITMENT→BOOK

The strongest questions are:

  • How much is AI currently being used within the organisation?
  • Where are staff losing time through repetitive or manual processes?
  • Which teams are you currently looking to develop?
  • Is there anybody you're looking to progress internally?
  • Are you likely to recruit into any of those teams over the next few months?
Then: “Let's get you booked in with one of our Partnership Managers to explore that properly.”
